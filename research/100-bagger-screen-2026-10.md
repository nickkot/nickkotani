# 100-Bagger Screen: US-listed small caps (as of 2026-10-12)

The criteria come from Christopher Mayer's *100 Baggers*. Themes: AI, health & fitness, experiences, outdoors / back-to-basics.

> **Read this first. Data quality caveats**
> - **Every figure comes from a cited web source, but only through search-result snippets.** Direct page fetches (sec.gov, IR sites, data aggregators) were blocked from the research environment, so nothing here was read directly from a 10-K or 10-Q. Before you buy, check every number against the primary filing.
> - **The research hit a web-search limit (200 per turn).** Items that couldn't be confirmed are marked **unverified**. In a few places I filled a gap from memory; those are marked **(memory, unverified)**.
> - **Market caps are from Aug–Oct 2026**, not today. Several of these stocks move 20%+ in a month.
> - **Calculations:** 5-yr CAGR = (FY2025 rev ÷ FY2020 rev)^(1/5) − 1, my arithmetic on the cited figures. "D/E" = total funded debt ÷ shareholders' equity. Lease liabilities are excluded unless stated.
> - This is a research screen, not investment advice.

## 1. Headline finding

I screened about 35 names: 6 AI, 7 health/fitness, 7 experiences, 8 outdoors, and 7 from a broad screen. **None passes all six criteria with fully verified data.**

- **Four come close** on the four hard, measurable tests (market cap, growth, founder plus insider ownership, gross margin plus debt): **Viant (DSP), Paysign (PAYS), Red Violet (RDVT) and Karooooo (KARO).** Each still has one unverified or borderline item.
- **The other six in the top 10 are near-misses.** Each fails one or two criteria, and every failure is flagged below.
- I've included them because you asked for 10. Read the list as **"4 genuine fits + 6 flagged near-misses"**, not 10 clean passes.

**Theme fit is a problem.** The companies that pass on the numbers are mostly not the ones that fit your daily-life themes. Of the outdoors names, 0 of 8 passed. Of the experiences names, 0 of 7 passed. Those businesses are mostly low gross margin (restaurants, cruise ships, consumer goods), debt- or lease-heavy, or no longer founder-run (§5). The criteria and your themes pull in different directions, and I've ranked by the criteria.

## 6. Where to dig deepest: three names and one question each

1. **Viant (DSP).** *Can Viant hold a gross margin of 45% or more while growing CTV share, as Amazon DSP and The Trade Desk compete on fees?* Check gross margin and take rate over the last 8 quarters in the 10-Qs. If margin falls while revenue grows, Viant is buying growth and the thesis weakens.
2. **Paysign (PAYS).** *What share of revenue comes from the largest plasma customers and the largest pharma copay programs, and when do those contracts renew?* The 10-K's customer-concentration note and the issuing-bank agreement answer this. While you're there, confirm Newcomer's founder status and the total insider stake from the proxy.
3. **Omada Health (OMDA, #5).** It fails the founder test, but it is the best match for your health/fitness theme. *Are employer and health-plan clients keeping and expanding Omada as GLP-1 use spreads, or replacing it with programs bundled by PBMs or drugmakers?* Look for net revenue retention, member growth per contract, and churn on the earnings calls. That one number tells you whether Omada complements GLP-1 drugs or gets replaced by them.

Runner-up: **Red Violet (RDVT)** has the cleanest fundamentals but the richest valuation (about 11–12x sales). Its question: *does the $100M raise go into acquisitions that keep EBITDA margins above 40%?*
