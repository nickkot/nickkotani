Research · October 2026

Hunting for 100-baggers

I screened about 35 US-listed small caps against Christopher Mayer's 100 Baggers criteria, looking through the lenses of AI, health & fitness, experiences, and the outdoors.

Criteria: market cap under $2B · revenue growth over 15%/yr for 5 years and still over 15% · founder running it with insiders owning over 10% · gross margin over 40% and debt/equity under 0.5 · room for 20x revenue in 15 years · reasonable valuation.

Headline: none pass all six with fully verified data. Four come close (DSP, PAYS, RDVT, KARO); the rest of the top 10 are flagged near-misses. All 8 outdoors names and all 7 experiences names failed.

Caveats: the figures come from web-search results, not checked against filings; market caps are from Aug–Oct 2026; some founder statuses are unverified. This is a personal research screen, not investment advice.

Three to dig into, and the one question for each

  1. Viant (DSP): can it hold a gross margin of about 45% or more while gaining connected-TV share, as Amazon and The Trade Desk compete on fees?
  2. Paysign (PAYS): how much revenue comes from the biggest plasma and pharma clients, and when do those contracts renew?
  3. Omada (OMDA): as GLP-1 drugs spread, are employers and health plans renewing and expanding Omada, or replacing it with bundled programs?

Themes that didn't make it

Outdoors (Solo Brands, Clarus, Johnson Outdoors, Arhaus, XPEL and others) and experiences (Lindblad, Sweetgreen, Lucky Strike, Travelzoo, GEN) failed on low gross margins, heavy debt or lease liabilities, slowing growth, or no founder at the helm. Klook and Strava aren't public yet, and Hims & Hers is well over $2B.
